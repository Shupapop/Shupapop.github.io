5G Phones in Malaysia — Do You Actually Need One?

Published September 2026  |  SpecShelf Editorial

5G has been around in Malaysia for a few years now — but is it actually worth paying more for? We cut through the hype and give you a straight answer.

Advertisement

First, What Is 5G?

5G is the fifth generation of mobile network technology. Compared to 4G LTE, it promises faster download speeds, lower latency (less lag), and the ability to connect more devices at once. In ideal conditions, 5G can reach speeds of over 1 Gbps — that's roughly 10–20 times faster than a typical 4G connection.

But "ideal conditions" is the key phrase here.

What Is the 5G Situation in Malaysia?

Malaysia's 5G rollout is handled through a single wholesale network operated by Digital Nasional Berhad (DNB), with major telcos like Maxis, Celcom Digi, U Mobile, and Telekom Malaysia offering 5G plans on top of it.

As of 2026, 5G coverage in Malaysia has expanded significantly in urban areas — particularly in the Klang Valley, Penang, Johor Bahru, and Kota Kinabalu. However, rural areas and smaller towns still have limited or no 5G coverage. If you live or work mainly outside city centres, you will likely still be on 4G most of the time, even with a 5G phone.

You can check your area's 5G coverage on your telco's official website before deciding.

Real-World 5G Speeds in Malaysia

In areas with good 5G signal, users in Malaysia typically see download speeds between 150 Mbps and 600 Mbps. This is noticeably faster than 4G, which usually delivers 20–80 Mbps in everyday use.

For everyday tasks — streaming videos, scrolling social media, video calls, browsing — 4G is already fast enough. The difference becomes more noticeable when downloading large files, streaming 4K video, or using cloud gaming services.

So, Do You Actually Need 5G?

Here's an honest breakdown:

You SHOULD get 5G if… You DON'T need 5G if…
You live or work in a major city with 5G coverage You're mostly in rural areas or small towns
You regularly download large files on mobile You mainly use Wi-Fi at home and work
You want your phone to stay relevant for 4–5 years You upgrade your phone every 1–2 years anyway
You use mobile data heavily every day You're on a tight budget and 4G phones are cheaper
You want the latest flagship or upper mid-range phone You only need a basic phone for calls and WhatsApp

Does 5G Drain Battery Faster?

Yes — connecting to 5G uses more power than 4G. However, most modern 5G phones handle this well with larger batteries and more efficient chipsets. Phones released in 2025 and 2026 on Snapdragon 7 or 8 series, or MediaTek Dimensity chips, manage 5G power consumption much better than early 5G devices did.

Many phones also let you switch between 5G and 4G manually in settings, so you can save battery when 5G isn't needed.

Is 5G Worth the Extra Cost?

Two years ago, you had to spend RM 2,000 or more to get a 5G phone. Today, 5G has come down significantly in price. There are solid 5G phones available in Malaysia starting from around RM 800–1,000, including options from Xiaomi, Redmi, Realme, and Samsung's A-series.

At this price point, the gap between 4G and 5G phones has narrowed enough that, if you're buying a new mid-range phone, it makes sense to get 5G — even if you're not in a 5G area yet. You'll be ready when coverage expands.

Our Verdict

If you're buying a new phone in 2026 and your budget allows it, go for 5G. The price gap has shrunk, 5G coverage in Malaysia is growing, and a 5G phone will serve you better over the next few years. But if you're on a very tight budget or live in an area with no 5G coverage, a good 4G phone is still perfectly capable for everyday use.

Don't let the hype pressure you — buy based on your actual needs and location.
